Welcome to The Harbors to Hills Greenway

The Harbors to Hills Greenway is a 11.77 mile long trail proposed in Fairfield, Connecticut. It will be directly adjacent to:

  • The Fairfield Metro-North station
  • Riverfield Elementary School
  • Mona Terrace senior center
  • Fairfield Enviornemtal Education Field Station (FEEFS)

It will also be within 1 mile of:

  • 13 of 16 of Fairfield's public schools
  • The geographic center of town

The trail offers Fairfielders a new, safe way to move through town.

The Route, section by section

Northern: Easton line at Wilson Road to CT-58

Summary

The greenway would link Fairfield to Easton (and the regional trail system) through the existing trail networks at Lake Mohegan, Grace Richardson, and Hoyden's Hill.

Ecology

The northern section of the greenway feature the transition between two EPA-designated level IV ecoregions: Southern New England Coastal Plains and Hills (region 59c) and Long Island Sound Coastal Lowland (region 59g). The transition's value is enhanced by the wide acreage and variety of habitats preserved.

History

Hemlock Reservoir was filled in 1914 by the Bridgeport Hydraulic Company in response to increased water demand in the region. The abandoned right of way is a former road with a stone foundation still visible. Additionally, large tulip trees (Liriodendron tulipifera), native meadows, and the recently installed beehives hint at Fairfield's onion farming heritage.

Existing Sections

Excitingly, most of this land is already preserved. Therefore, it is already possible to explore miles of this section in Lake Mohegan, Grace Richardson, and Hoyden's Hill open spaces.

Future Work

The most significant change in this region is to restore the right of way northwest of Hoyden's Hill Road; this would require brush clearing, boardwalks, and a small bridge over East Cricker Brook. Next, a small trail alongside Smith Richardson Golf course and its continuation along a 19 foot easement would link the greenway between the right of way and Grace Richardson via an existing trail network in the Centennial Watershed State Forest.


Transition: CT-58 to Duck Farm Road

Summary

The Greenway passes CT-58 (Black Rock Turnpike) and takes a detour from the Mill River for dramatic scenery in Samp Mortar and Mountain Laurel Open Spaces. It also crosses Mill Plain road at this point, offering opportunities to make busy intersections safer.

Ecology

Mountain Laurel (Kalmia latifolia, CT state flower) blooms in late spring display Connecticut's natural beauty. This section also contains many American chestnut seedlings, which have become rare due to chestnut blight. The blight is an introduced fungal pest that wiped out over 99% of all chestnuts - but trees with resistance have been planted at Springer Glen open space.

History

Samp Mortar Open Space is also notable for the hollows dug into the rocks, evidence of precolonial habitation of Fairfield by the indigenous peoples of the area.

Existing Sections

This section of the greenway already features several small trail networks, and their impact on public safety and appreciation of conservation would be greatly increased by linking them.

Future Work

This section of the greenway will likely follow the immediate signage for a prolonged time, as various portions of the "Grow/Transform" route require small land donations/conservation easements, mostly in the wetland/upland review areas of existing single family homes. While more challenging to implement, this section passes only a few hundred feet from the geographic center of Fairfield, emphasizing its importance to community movement.


Central: Duck Farm Road to the Post Road

The Harbors to Hills Greenway passes through the heart of town along a series of existing open spaces by the Mill River until Mill Hollow, at which point it heads east to pass under Interstate 95 at Mill Plain Road. Here, the greenway will link with the Fairfield train station, increasing accessibility and rendering the greenway eligible for transit oriented grants. Additionally, the greenway can be a fantastic first impression for those visiting Fairfield by train or I-95, potentially making our town more attractive to investment. Ecologically, it also represents a chance to extend the pollinator pathway through a densely populated area, and serve as a launchpad for invasive species removal. Despite its more urban nature, this section of the greenway only needs two major improvements: the acquisition of the Braun parcel and the completion of a pedestrian bridge north of the northern millpond in Perry's Millponds. This has been repeatedly suggested since 1987. Inaction risks further destabilizing the river and its sensitive ecological profile by forcing pedestrians through the river. The recent restoration and dedication of the Joy Shaw Trail is the first implemented section of the greenway (November 2025).


Southern: Post Road to South Pine Creek Beach

The Harbors to Hills Greenway's southern terminus features a remarkably well restored salt marsh spanning hundreds of acres. The range of flora found in this area spans from pockets of oak-hickory (Quercus-Carya spp.) forests to low marshes. In between, rare communities such as gray birch (Betula populifolia) swamps demonstrate the vulnerability of this area to sea level rise and invasive species. The fauna, meanwhile, range from the Dekay's snake (Storeria dekayi) to fiddler crabs (Minuca spp.) and snowy egrets (Egretta thula). The educational value of the trail becomes clear when comparing this section of the trail to the northern section, as the entire width of the EPA Level IV ecoregion 59g (Long Island Sound Coastal Lowland) has been traversed in approximately 13 miles. Most of the trail's final two miles are in the best condition of the entire route: crushed gravel intended to allow for greater accessibility. After crossing by Kiwanis Fields, the new realignment of Serenity Path southwest to Pine Creek Avenue to exit at South Pine Creek Beach, rewarding trail users with a stunning view of Long Island in the distance. This is the first section of trail that has been updated in response to this proposal, with a projected completion by the end of 2025. The other significant proposed changes are in the northernmost portions of this section, where the connection of a conservation easement at 185 Thorpe Street and an existing trail in the Oldfield North Marsh Open Space would allow the greenway to avoid South Pine Creek Road entirely and reduce its length on the US-1 to the intersection at Mill Plain and Thorpe Street.

The Timeline

2020

Fairfield resident Joseph Walewski runs every street of Fairfield as a COVID project, and realizes how many open spaces lie within 1/2 mile of each other.


2024

Winter

Joseph Walewski attends January's town hall on Santacon and advocates for stronger environmental protections for Fairfield's beaches in response to the littering at the 2023 rendition of the event.

Fall

Joseph Walewski proposes trails linking the open spaces along the Mill River and Pine Creek at the town's Plan of Conservation and Development public comment session in October. After the proposal was met with enthusiasm, he meets Town Planning and Zoning Comission Jeff Randolph, who encourages him to begin designing the route.


2025

Spring

Jeff Randolph introduces Joseph Walewski to Alex Ressi, a member of the bike and pedestrian committee who had independently begun designing a nearly identical route. The three of them hold several meetings to draft a plan forward.

Summer

The bike and pedestrian committee unanimously approves an endorsement of the proposed route for future planning.

The conservation commission takes action on Serenity Path, a privately maintained trail in the Pine Creek open space. Joseph Walewski advocates for it to stay open as the future greenway relies on it to reach the beach. The commission plans to re-route it to stay on town land for its entire length.

Late first selectman Gill Gerber hosts a cross-department meeting to plan the greenway during one of his last days in office. There, Parks and Recreation department director Anthony Calabrese coins the name "Harbor to Hills".

Fall

The first new section of the greenway, the Joy Shaw Trail, is completed in November. The trail is named after Joy Shaw, who advocated for Perry's Millponds' preservation in 1967, creating the 70 acre open space we know today. The second section, the realignment of Serenity Path, is completed in late November.


2026

Spring

The Bike and Pedestrian Committee begins the application process for a planning grant and is pursuing letters of approval for other community stakeholders. Such a planning grant would provide the necessary resources to create site specfic engineering plans for road and river crossings.

Summer

The Bike and Pedestrian Committee begins hosting walks along future segments. These walks are at 9am on the last Saturday of the month, with the location posted in First Selectwoman Christine Vitale's newsletters.

The Funding

State Government

Open Space and Watershed Land Acquisition Grant Program

The Open Space and Watershed Land Acquisition Grant Program provides funding to municipalities, land trusts, and other non-profit organizations for the acquisition of land for conservation purposes. The program is administered by the Connecticut Department of Energy and Environmental Protection (DEEP) and offers grants for the purchase of land, conservation easements, and other related expenses. The Harbors to Hills Greenway project could potentially apply for funding through this program to acquire land or conservation easements necessary for the development of the greenway.


Town Government

20% Match

The aforementioned state grants require a 20% match, which the town can provide through a combination of in-kind contributions (such as volunteer labor for trail construction and maintenance) and cash contributions from the town's budget (via approval of the representative town meeting).

Land Acquisition Fund

Town law requires developers to donate 10% of the land or funds equal to 10% of the land's value when creating a new subdivision.


Other Avenues

Conservation 10K

A proposed road race that could raise funds for the greenway project. Additionally, portions of the race will be on the Frank Rice Trail, an existing portion of the greenway that can inspire runners about the rest of the route.

How You Can Help

Advocate for the Greenway

Contact your town representatives (RTM) and state house / senate delegation and express your support for the Harbors to Hills Greenway project. Attend Conservation Department/ Bike & Pedestrian / Town Planning & Zoning (TPZ) / Parks and Recreation meetings, participate in public comment sessions, and engage with local government officials to advocate for the greenway's development and funding.


Volunteer for Trail Maintenance

Once sections of the greenway are established, volunteer opportunities will arise for trail maintenance, invasive species removal, and habitat restoration. Get involved with local conservation organizations or community groups such as the town of Fairfield Conservation Department, the Connecticut Audubon Society, the Aspetuck Land Trust, and others that are working on the greenway project to contribute your time and effort towards its upkeep.


Donate to the Project

Consider making a financial contribution to support the Harbors to Hills Greenway project. Donations can be made directly to the town's conservation department to help fund land acquisition, trail construction, and ongoing maintenance.
